RHA and BA both agricultural waste products, are implemented as filler substances in asphalt pavement formulations in this examination. The primary objective involves assessing the influence of utilizing these agricultural residues as filler components. This application of agricultural waste- based fillers is an effort to support environmentally friendly filler material utilization [14]. 2 Materials and methods This research employs a quantitative methodology involving experimental procedures on multiple test samples to achieve target material compositions via laboratory examinations compliant with Indonesian National Standards. The methodology consists of several main stages, including material testing, mix design without filler, mixture development incorporating Rice Husk Ash (RHA) and sugarcane Bagasse Ash (BA) fillers, and determination of the optimal composition for each mixture variation. 2.1 Material The materials used include coarse and fine aggregates combined with a filler and mixed with 60/70 penetration grade asphalt. The filler variations using RHA and BA are added at 0%, 2%, 4%, 6%, and 8% of the total weight (passing #200 sieve aggregate). The chemical composition of both ashes plays a pivotal role in determining their behavior in asphalt mixtures. RHA is known for its high SiO2 content, while BA contains more CaO and MgO, contributing to better binding reactivity [14, 15]. Figure 1 and Figure 2.
