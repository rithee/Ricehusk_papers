The sand content of normal concrete mixtures is usually 35–50%, being typically lower than stone content. In contrast, RCC mixtures usually have higher sand content of about 50–55%, while stone content can be below 40%. As mentioned earlier, the binder content of RCC mixtures is also lower than that of normal concretes. The mini- mum cementitious content for RCC pavements is typically 250 kg/m3 or 10%, whereas that of normal concretes is higher, being typically in the range of 10–15% (FHWA, 2016; Harrington et al., 2010; Ram- babu et al., 2023). Interestingly, studies have also been done to investigate RCC mixtures of very low 80–160 kg/m3 cementitious content (Villena et al., 2011). Another crucial mix design aspect that dis- tinguishes RCC from normal concrete, is water content. For normal concrete, water content is a major variable that is adjusted in mix design along with cement content, to obtain the required workability 368 S. O. EKOLU ET AL. and concrete strength. In contrast, the amount of water used in RCC mixtures is not prescribed in a similar manner as it is done for normal concrete. Rather, the water content used in RCC mixtures is a fixed quantity determined as the optimum moisture content (OMC) based on the moisture–density relationship obtained using the soil–compaction approach (ASTM D1557, 2012). The mixtures of RCC comprised CEM I 52.5 N Portland cement containing 0%, 5%, 10%, 20% and 30% wt FA or RHA. Various aggregate characteristics were measured to determine their suitability for road applications. Compaction tests were done to obtain values of optimum moisture content for RCC mixtures. The mechanical properties investigated comprised compressive strength, static modulus of elasticity, dynamic modulus of elasticity and ultrasonic pulse velocity. Results showed that proportions of up to 5–10% wt RHA or FA were optimal and can be recommended for incorporation in RCC pavement mixtures. Specifications The UK national standards for highways, for example, have developed a design chart for estimating the concrete thickness layer of RCC pavements (National Highways UK, 2021). For quality control, the minimum compressive strength of 50 or 5 MPa minimum flexural strength, is specified as the basis for layer thickness design (UK Government, 2021). According to CSRA specification for concrete roads, a minimum 28–day compressive strength of 38 MPa or corresponding flexural strength of 3.8 MPa, is considered adequate to provide sufficient load-bearing capacity and abrasion resistance (CSRA, 1987). Generally, the 28–day compressive strength of 28–41 MPa, along with flexural strength values of 3.5–7 MPa are typically used or specified. It is suggested in the literature that high-performing RCC pavements should typically attain compressive and flexural strengths of 40 and 4.5 MPa, respectively (Rambabu et al., 2023; Selvam et al., 2022).
