The main component of RHA is silicon dioxide (SiO2) generated from biomass, and the silica properties of the material are greatly influenced by the processing conditions. Cristobalite and tridymite, two less reactive crystalline silica minerals, are frequently found in ash from uncontrolled combustion, whereas burning RHA between 500 and 700 °C produces the largest amount of amorphous silica [6]. Because of its porous particle structure and high amorphous silica content, RHA has a wide surface area that contributes to its reactivity. Finer ash further enhances reactivity. RHA is a pozzolanic substance that can replace up to 15% of Portland cement; fine RHA, in particular, can reduce mortar porosity and improve compressive strength. RHA also increases the longevity of concrete by fortifying it against sulphate degradation, water permeability, and chloride ion penetration. In recent years, there has been a substantial increase in the attention given to the utilisation of ecologically friendly components, such as rice husk ash (RHA) and silica fume (SF), in the production of concrete. In their study, Zareei et al. (2017) determined that the most effective amounts of SF and RHA to substitute cement in pastes and concretes were 10% by weight. The strength of the concretes was unaffected by the addition of up to 30% of SF or 15% of ground RHA, despite the fresh mixtures showing a noticeable reduction in slump.
