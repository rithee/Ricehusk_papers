Table 3, the IA values for 3D-printed plain concrete and rubber powder specimens are presented. Theoretically, IA ranges from 0 to positive infinity. A higher IA value indicates a more significant anisotropic effect on compressive strength caused by the layer-by-layer printing process and the incorporation of rubber powder. When IA = 0, it suggests that the compressive strength is unaffected by either the printing process or the incorporation of rubber powder. Therefore, investigating the influence of rubber powder size and replacement ratio on IA is beneficial for optimizing the material formulation and structural performance of 3D-printed rubber powder concrete. As depicted in Fig. 10, the particle size and replacement ratio of rubber powder considerably affect the IA of the compressive strength in 3D-printed concrete. For plain concrete, the IA value is 0.288. After adding 5% rubber powder, the IA decreases, with the most pronounced reduction observed for the 20-mesh mixture, where IA drops from 0.288 to 0.134. When the rubber powder size is reduced to 40 mesh and 80 mesh, the IA values become only slightly lower than that of plain concrete, indicating a relatively limited change in compressive anisotropy. This can be explained by the fact that, at the same replacement ratio, the 20-mesh mixture contains the fewest rubber particles dispersed in the mortar. With fewer inclusions, the hydrophobic surface characteristics of the 20-mesh rubber may promote paste redistribution and facilitate the filling of printing-induced voids, thereby enhancing interlayer and inter­ filament bonding.
