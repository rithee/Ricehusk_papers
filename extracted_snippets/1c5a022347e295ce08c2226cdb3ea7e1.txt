These birds do not build their own nests; instead, they lay their eggs in the nests of other species, depending on the host birds for incubation. Sometimes, the host may recognize these foreign eggs and either reject them or abandon the entire nest. Cuckoo eggs generally hatch sooner than those of the host species, and after hatching, the cuckoo chicks mimic the calls of the host’s young to mislead the mother bird, ensuring they receive more food by pushing out other eggs from the nest. This unique reproductive strategy serves as a conceptual model for the cuckoo search algorithm. The algorithm’s key steps, inspired by cuckoo behavior, are as follows [44]: First, it randomly generates a collection of potential solutions, resembling how cuckoo eggs are spread among various nests. Then, certain solutions are discarded, similar to cuckoos changing nests and laying eggs elsewhere, while introducing new solutions in different locations within the search space.
