Abstract. Supplemental cementitious material has been commonly added 
to concrete in recent years. By replacing some of the Ordinary Portland 
Cement (OPC) in concrete with cementitious elements like fly ash and 
silica fume, the mechanical properties of the mixture can be enhanced. 
There is an impending fly ash shortage in Arkansas because to nearby coal 
plant businesses. The possible use of rice husk ash (RHA) to replace 
cementitious materials has been investigated in this study. RHA is a waste 
product that is produced during agricultural processes. Due to its elevated 
silica content, it can be used as cementitious materials. In this 
investigation, concrete was modified to partially replace Ordinary Portland 
Cement using silica fume (0 to 20%) and RHA (0 to 30 %) at different 
percentages. In this investigation, a Class C fly ash that is often utilised 
locally was also included for comparison analysis with concrete specimens 
modified with RHA and silica fume. Both the fresh concrete mix's and 
hardened concrete's mechanical characteristics were ascertained through 
laboratory testing. The durability of the material will be improved, and the 
quantity of cement required will be reduced by the addition of silica fume 
and RHA to concrete. This includes improvements in water absorption, 
sorpitivity, effective porosity, permeability, acid resistance, and resistance 
to fast chloride penetration. Nevertheless, the incorporation of silica fume 
and RHA in concrete leads to an elevation in water demands in comparison 
to the standard regular Portland cement mixture.  
1 Introduction 
 
Cement manufacture, especially the clinker formation process, releases a large amount 
of CO2 into the atmosphere and uses a lot of energy, which exacerbates the greenhouse 
effect and contributes to global warming. As an additional cementitious ingredient, fly ash 
and granulated blast furnace slag, two byproducts of agricultural and industrial activities, 
help reduce environmental issues. A reduction in cement manufacturing leads to a drop in 
energy usage and carbon dioxide emissions. In addition to this, the mechanical qualities of 
concrete mixtures are improved by these chemicals, and the resistance to penetration is also 
increased. A significant portion of amorphous SiO2 is present in siliceous materials

into cement matrices, they exhibit chemical reactions that are analogous to one another. 
Cement-based products gain strength due to their heightened reactivity, resulting from 
pozzolanic interactions with calcium hydroxide produced during cement hydration. 
The addition of silica fume and rice husk ash to concrete results in enhanced durability, 
increased strength, reduced permeability, and improved workability. To achieve these 
benefits, these materials can be included in concrete mixtures as partial replacements for 
cement. Because silica fume particles are so small, they can fill in the gaps left by cement 
particles, making concrete that is more compact and less porous. Conversely, rice husk ash 
includes active silica, which can enhance the overall effectiveness of concrete mixtures. 
Because they have both shown to increase the durability and life of concrete, rice husk ash 
and silica fume are useful additions to building projects. Moreover, the inclusion of these 
elements in concrete mixtures can further promote sustainable practices by making use of 
waste materials from other industries. This not only diminishes the ecological footprint of 
construction projects but also aids in the preservation of natural resources for future 
generations. In summary, incorporating silica fume and rice husk ash into concrete mixtures 
can result in the construction of more robust and long-lasting structures that are also eco-
friendly. By integrating these materials, construction projects can simultaneously realise 
enhancements in performance and meet environmental objectives. 
 
The by-product of smelting ferrosilicon or metallic silicon in an electric arc furnace is 
silica fume (SF), a finely divided non-crystalline silica used in the alloys industry. High-
purity quartz undergoes a reduction to silicon at very high temperatures during the process, 
producing SiO2 vapours that oxidise and condense into small particles that contain 85%–
99% amorphous silica [1-4]. These particles have approximately spherical forms and 
typically have diameters between 20 and 500 nm. The estimated yearly production of SF 
worldwide is one to 1.5 million tonnes. SF-containing concrete is well known for being 
stronger and more resilient than regular Portland cement concrete, especially in sulfate-rich 
regions. 
 
The residue that remains after burning rice husks the outer shells of paddy rice that are 
removed during husking is known as rice husk ash (RHA). One tonne of paddy rice burns 
to about 40 kg of ash. This ash is made when ortho silicic acid from groundwater is 
ingested by rice plants and polymerizes to form amorphous silica in the husks. Organic 
molecules burn to produce carbon dioxide, leaving silica behind in the ash[5]. The main 
component of RHA is silicon dioxide (SiO2) generated from biomass, and the silica 
properties of the material are greatly influenced by the processing conditions. Cristobalite 
and tridymite, two less reactive crystalline silica minerals, are frequently found in ash from 
uncontrolled combustion, whereas burning RHA between 500 and 700 °C produces the 
largest amount of amorphous silica [6]. Because of its porous particle structure and high 
amorphous silica content, RHA has a wide surface area that contributes to its reactivity. 
Finer ash further enhances reactivity. RHA is a pozzolanic substance that can replace up to 
15% of Portland cement; fine RHA, in particular, can reduce mortar porosity and improve 
compressive strength. RHA also increases the longevity of concrete by fortifying it against 
sulphate degradation, water permeability, and chloride ion penetration.  
 
In recent years, there has been a substantial increase in the attention given to the 
utilisation of ecologically friendly components, such as rice husk ash (RHA) and silica 
fume (SF), in the production of concrete. In their study, Zareei et al. (2017) determined that 
the most effective amounts of SF and RHA to substitute cement in pastes and concretes 
were 10% by weight. The strength of the concretes was unaffected by the addition of up to 
30% of SF or 15% of ground RHA, despite the fresh mixtures showing a noticeable 
reduction in slump. According to these results, concrete's mechanical properties can be 
positively impacted by using both SF and RHA as partial cement substitutes [7]. Xu et al.

substantial enhancements in the mechanical characteristics of concrete.[9]. Overall, the 
findings from the literature review suggest that concrete incorporating RHA and SF has the 
potential to meet the strength and durability requirements of construction materials while 
offering environmental benefits through the use of sustainable materials[10-14]. However, 
further research is needed to fully explore the optimal utilization of RHA and SF in 
concrete mixtures and their long-term performance in various environmental conditions. 
2 Materials  
2.1 Binding Materials  
 
For the preparation of the specimens in this investigation, silica fume and regular 
Portland cement grade 43 were utilised. The cement's specific gravity, consistency, and first 
setting time were determined to be 3.215 and 31%, respectively. When compared to other 
concrete admixtures, silica fume is often regarded as one of the most effective ones for 
regular cement concrete. The objective of this experiment was to improve the compressive 
strength of concrete by substituting 0 to 20% of conventional Portland cement with silica 
fume. 
 
In this study, Portland cement is replaced by silica fume and RHA. Table 1 displays the 
physical characteristics of the binders, as determined through an assessment conducted in 
compliance with the Indian Standard IS 4031. The experimental materials used in this 
study, silica fume and RHA, have larger particle sizes and higher specific surface areas 
compared to Portland cement, as indicated by their physical characteristics. Silica fume has 
a specific surface area of 200 m2/kg and a particle size of 10 μm, while RHA has a specific 
surface area of 170 m2/kg and a particle size of 25 μm. In contrast, Portland cement has a 
specific surface area of 320 m2/kg and a particle size ranging from 65 to 90 μm. Comparing 
these results to Portland cement, they show that both silica fume and RHA are highly 
refined and do not have a crystalline structure. Furthermore, compared to Portland cement, 
the specific gravities of the binders show that silica fume and RHA have lower weight 
densities.

4 Result and discussion 
4.1 Compressive Strength  
 
The Figure 2 demonstrates the significant impact of substituting silica fume and RHA 
on the compressive strength of concrete at 7 and 28 days. After a period of 28 days, the 
compressive strength of M1 was measured to be 31.6 N/mm2. In comparison, the concrete 
mixes for M2, M3, M4, M5, and M6, which contained different percentages of silica fume 
and RHA, had compressive strengths of 33 N/mm2, 34 N/mm2, 38 N/mm2, 42 N/mm2, and 
40 N/mm2 respectively. After thorough examination, it was seen that the compressive 
strength of the specimens rose proportionally with the level of substitution of the binding 
substance. On the other hand, there was an increase in compressive strength when thirty 
percent of the binding material was substituted with RHA. It was discovered that the 
compressive strength of every single concrete mix was higher than the specified strength 
value, with the exception of the mixes that had considerable amounts of silica components..

5 Conclusion  
The According to the preliminary research on the effects of RHA in high strength 
concrete with silica fume, the ensuing end may be suffocating.  
 
 
The addition of silica fume to RHA concrete increases its flexural and 
compressive strengths. 20% silica fume replacement lowers the compressive 
strength.  
 
The concrete matrix becomes denser as a result of silica fume, which increases its 
ability to support loads and prevents cracking when loads are applied. 
 
The concrete matrix's pores are filled with silica fume, which lowers the material's 
permeability to water and harsh chemicals. 
 
RHA concrete is more durable as a result of its reduced permeability, particularly 
in areas where moisture intrusion or chemical attacks are common. 
 
The interaction between silica fume and calcium hydroxide through the pozzolanic 
reaction strengthens the concrete structure, enhancing its durability against 
weathering and chemical corrosion.
