A B S T R A C T   
Concrete is a heterogeneous, multiphase, and multilayer composite system with extremely intricate microscopic 
pore structure. The structural characteristics of microscopic pore of concrete are closely related to its mechanical 
properties and durability. The pore structure of concrete with various strength grades (C10-C150) was analyzed 
by Mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) from the aspects of pore diameter distribution, pore volume, pore 
specific surface area and fractal feature. The Menger sponge model was used to calculate the fractal dimension of 
different pore regions in each test group, and the most suitable pore fractal region was selected to establish the 
mathematical model between fractal dimension and compressive strength. The results showed that all fractal 
curves are divided into four segments according multi-fractal characteristic of concrete, namely Region I, Region 
II, Region III and Region IV. Region I is macropores fractal region (d = 10 μm ~ 950 μm), representing the 
packing patterns of hydrated cement grains, while Region II - Region Ⅳ is micropores fractal region (d = 0.0064 
μm ~ 10 μm), reflecting mainly the microstructure of C-S-H hydrates. Region I and Region Ⅳ exhibit obvious 
fractal features and the fractal dimension D is between 2 and 3. However, the fractal dimension D > 3 in Region 
II and Region III, which are nonphysical from the point of view of surface geometry. The fractal dimension can 
clearly describe the internal pore characteristic, and it is a real good correlation with the most probable pore 
diameter and the pore specific surface area. Moreover, the mathematical model between the fractal dimension 
and the compressive strength both accords with the positive power exponential function in Region I and Region 
IV. The fractal dimension can be viewed as a comprehensive parameter of pore shape and spatial distribution of 
pore structure, which can more accurately represent the relationship between the microscopic pore structure 
features and compressive strength of concrete. In practical engineering, it serves to realize the damage detection 
of concrete on site.   
1. Introduction 
Concrete has become the most widely used building material today 
due to its superior performance and wide range of materials [1]. Con­
crete is a heterogeneous, multiphase, and multilayer composite system 
with extremely complex microscopic pore structure [2]. The macro­
scopic characteristics of concrete are the reflection of its complexity 
microstructural, such as irregularity, indeterminacy, and nonlinearity. 
And the structural characteristics of macroscopic agglomerations and 
microscopic pore of concrete are closely related to its mechanical 
properties and durability. For instance, mechanical properties, density, 
thermal conductivity, volume stability, hygroscopicity and permeability 
of materials are directly or indirectly related to its porosity. Moreover, 
the shape, diameter size, surface area and spatial distribution of pores 
are also influencing factors [3-7]. However, there are different pores 
diameter in concrete, such as residual bubbles, capillary pores, gel pore 
and fine cracks caused by drying shrinkage, which results in random 
disorder and diversity of the morphology and spatial distribution of 
pores, and makes the micro-structure of cement composites become 
abnormal chaotic and complex which traditional Euclidean geometry 
cannot accurately describe. According to fractal theory, the pores of 
porous materials have obvious fractal features in its pore volume, pore 
surface area and pore size distribution [8-13]. Fractal dimension is a 
parameter that can be used to mark the complexity of a fractal set, and 
each fractal set corresponds to a fractal dimension defined in some way.

basic point that distinguishes it from classical geometry. The fractal 
dimension can be used to describe the complexity of the morphology 
and spatial distribution of pores when the fractal theory is applied to 
represent the microscopic pore structure of concrete. The larger the 
fractal dimension, the more complex of the morphology and spatial 
distribution of pores in concrete. Studies have shown that [14,15], the 
fractal dimension can play a bridge role between the microstructure and

Previous studies have shown that the concrete contains a large 
number of pores and microcracks of different orders, which are very 
similar to the sponge [18,19]. In addition, the construction principle of 
the Menger sponge is similar to the measuring principle of MIP. 
Therefore, the fractal model of Menger sponge can be utilized to simu­
late the pore characteristics between material particles. Many re­
searchers have studied the pore structure of cement-based compound 
cementitious materials and established a fractal calculation model based 
on the construction principle of the Menger sponge model [9,18-23]. 
Friesen [22] constructed the surface fractal model of porous materials 
based on the test results of MIP and the construction principle of Menger 
sponge, and calculated the pore surface ractal dimension of of porous 
materials. Ji et al. [23] studied the relationship between pore volume 
and pore size, and established a calculation model for the fractal 
dimension of pore volume. And Ji considered that the construction 
process of the Menger sponge is analogous to the process of filling pores 
with the hydration product of compound cementitious materials. 
At present, although some researchers have studied the range of 
fractal dimension and the relationship between fractal dimension and 
mechanical properties of concrete [19,24-27]. But the conclusions 
reached are quite different and some are even opposite [20,25,26]. 
According to the current research, it is found that there is still not ac­
curate conclusion on the reasonable range of fractal dimension of 
microscopic pore structure and the relationship between fractal 
dimension and strength in concrete. In addition, there is little systematic 
expression in the mathematical model that represents the relationship 
between microscopic pore structure parameters and compressive 
strength beyond the tensile or the combined strength of a particular 
material. Therefore, this paper constructs a reasonable fractal model 
based on the microscopic pore structure characteristics of concrete. The 
pore structure of 11 kinds of concrete with different strength grades was 
analyzed by mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) on the aspects of pore 
diameter distribution, pore volume, specific surface area and fractal 
feature. According to the multifractal nature of the fractal curve, the 
Menger sponge model was used to calculate the fractal dimension of 
different pore stages in each test group. Then, multiple characterization 
parameters of pore structure were considered include the pore volume, 
pore diameter distribution and specific surface area, the optimal pa­
rameters were selected to establish the mathematical model between 
microscopic pore structure and compressive strength. 
2. Materials and experiments 
2.1. Raw materials 
The raw materials used in concrete include P⋅O 42.5 Portland cement 
(C) and silica fume (SF), aggregates and water. The chemical composi­
tions of cement and silica fume are exhibited in Table 1. And the 
physical properties of cement and silica fume are listed in Table 2 and 
Table 3. Aggregates consist of crushed limestone with particle size of 
5–20 mm and river sand with fineness modulus of 2.8. Clean tap water 
and polycarboxylate superplasticizer with 19.1% water reducing rate 
are applied in mixing. 
2.2. Mixture proportions and preparation of specimens 
For concrete, the water-binder ratio (W/C) is one of the most 
important factors affecting its compressive strength. Table 4 shows the 
mix proportion of 11 different strength grades concrete, which based on 
the Chinese national standard “Specification for mix proportion design 
of ordinary concrete (JGJ 55–2011)” [28]. 
Table 5 shows the forming and curing methods of specimens, ac­
cording to a Chinese Standard ‘‘Standard for test method of mechanical 
properties on ordinary concrete (GBT50081-2002)” [29]. 
2.3. Mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) 
The Mercury intrusion porosimetry (MIP) of concrete specimens 
after curing 7, 28, 90 days were conducted by PoreMaster 33 mercury 
porosimeter in the pressure range of 0.23 ~ 33000 Psia, which measured 
pores diameters was 0.0064 ~ 950 μm. Besides, the contact angle be­
tween the mercury and the pore surface was θ = 130◦. The size of MIP 
fragment specimens was 3–5 mm approximately, taken from the con­
crete specimen cores. Before tested, MIP specimens were placed in ab­
solute ethanol for at least 24 h to dehydrate and terminate hydration. 
Then, the specimens were put into the oven under 60℃ until it achieved 
constant mass to evaporate the free water and alcohol. 
2.4. Compressive strength experiment 
The compressive strength experiment was carried out at curing ages 
of 3 days, 7 days, 28 days, 90 days according to a Chinese Standard 
‘‘Standard for test method of mechanical properties on ordinary concrete 
(GBT50081-2002)” [29]. Triplicate specimens were tested for each 
concrete mixture, and their average value were taken as the final value 
of the strength.

4. Results and discussion 
4.1. Pore diameter distribution 
Fig. 2 and Fig. 3 shows that the incremental and cumulative pore 
diameter distribution curves of each test group at 7 days, 28 days and 90 
days. The most probable pore diameter and the threshold pore diameter 
are used to characterize the changes in the microscopic internal pore 
structure of concrete in Fig. 2 and Fig. 3, respectively. The most probable 
pore diameter which is the pore diameter of the largest number of pores 
in the concrete, while the threshold pore diameter is the pore diameter 
when the pore volume in concrete increases significantly. The curves 
move to the left with the decrease of W/C and the pore diameter dis­
tribution range of C10-C150 concrete varies significantly. At 7 days, the 
most probable diameter of C10 concrete is close to that of C20 concrete, 
which is 1.6, 2.5 and 3.8 times of C60, C80, and C150 concrete 
respectively. Meanwhile, the threshold pore diameter of C10 and C20 
concrete is between 300 nm and 400 nm, while C60-C80 concrete is 
between 10 nm and 200 nm and C100-150 concrete is between 50 nm 
and 100 nm. It is caused by excess water forming many pores inside the 
concrete when the W/C is too large. At 28 days and 90 days, the change 
trend of pore diameter distribution between C10-C150 concrete is the 
same as that of 7 days. Moreover, the peak position moves to the left and 
the peak value of each curve decreases gradually over time, in Fig. 2. 
This phenomenon means that the most probable diameter and the 
number of pores in the concrete reduces. Meanwhile, the cumulative 
pore volume and the threshold pore diameter decrease with curing ages, 
according to Fig. 3. It indicates that the pore structure refines gradually 
in the concrete with the progress of hydration. With the curing age, 
water and cement in concrete react to generate a large number of hy­
dration products, such as C-S-H gel and Ca(OH)2, which are wrapped on 
the surface of unhydrated cement particles to form a dense internal 
microstructure. At the same time, the unhydrated cement particles also 
play a role in filling the internal pores of concrete. In the later stage of 
hydration, with the increase of the density of pore structure, the growth 
space of cement particles is lack, and unhydrated the cement particles 
hydrated in situ to form very dense hydration products. Therefore, the 
coarse pores in concrete, microcracks at the interface between aggregate 
and cement paste gradually disappear, and denser pore structure is 
formed inside the concrete. Note that the difference in pore diameter 
distribution between C10-C150 concrete decreases, at 28 days and 90 
days. In addition, this may be due to the hydration process of C10-C150 
concrete gradually tends to be stable after 28 days. 
4.2. Pore volume distribution 
The pore volume distribution of concrete is shown in Fig. 4. The 
effect of porosity at each pore grade on strength is different. According 
to the porosity at each pore grade in concrete and their influence coef­
ficient, the pore diameter is finely divided into four categories, as fol­
lows: harmless pores (d < 20 nm), little harmful pores (d = 20 nm–50 
nm), harmful pores (d = 50 nm–200 nm), and much harmful pores(d >
200 nm) by Wu [32]. At 28 days, the total pore volume of C30 and C60 
concrete is 1.72 times and 1.37 times that of C150 concrete, respec­
tively. The volume of much harmful pores and harmful holes in C30 
concrete accounted for 25.77% and 14.43 % of its total volume, 
respectively, while accounted for 12.28 % and 11.40 % in C150 con­
crete. The same conclusion can be drawn from Fig. 2 and Fig. 3, the 
smaller the W/C ratio is, the denser the pore structure. The water in 
concrete is used as a basic medium for dissolving the cementitious ma­
terials during the hydration, and then participates in the reaction as a 
reactant. When the W/C is larger, the amount of cementitious material is 
less, which leads the cementitious material cannot completely wrap the 
surface of the aggregate. At this time, free water wraps the surface of fine 
aggregate, causing many pores to form inside concrete under unbal­
anced the action of pore water pressure. It will lead to the adjacent small 
pores connected into larger holes, forming a water-bleeding channel. 
Ultimately, the porous structure of concrete is formed. To a certain 
extent, it affects the pore diameter distribution of concrete. Moreover, 
with the increase of curing ages, the total pore volume and the volume of 
much harmful pores gradually decrease, while the volume of harmless 
pores gradually increases. The proportion of much harmful pores vol­
ume in total pore volume of C60 concrete from 27.61% at 7 days to 
17.79% at 28 days, and 15.15% at 90 days, while the harmless pores 
from 20.20% at 7d to 28.15% at 28 days, and 39.06% at 90 days. The 
hydration process of C10-C150 concrete gradually stabilizes after 28

4.3. Specific surface area distribution 
Fig. 5 Display the specific surface area of C10-C150 concrete at 7 
days, 28 days, and 90 days. The harmless pores dominate the total 
specific surface area in all cases, followed by the little harmful pores. It 
reveals that the specific surface area depends largely on the volume of

the mesopores and macropores. In addition, with the decrease of W/C, 
the total specific surface area and the specific surface area of little 
harmful pores shows an increasing trend. The proportion of harmless 
pore volume in the total specific surface area of C30, C60 and C150

43.41% at 28 days, and 45.01%, 47.11% and 53.61% at 90 days. From 
Fig. 2- Fig. 4, the pore volume of the harmless pores increases with the 
decrease of W/C, while the pore diameter decreases. It leads to an in­
crease in the roughness of pore surface area. However, the much harmful 
pores and harmful pores have no obvious change trend. Although, the 
pore volume of the harmless pores increases with the decrease of W/C, 
the pore diameter distribution range concentrates in the area with 
smaller values. The above phenomenon shows that the pore volume and 
pore diameter decide jointly the value of the pore specific surface area. 
When the pores volume is constant, the pore specific surface area in­
creases with of pore diameter reduces. Therefore, the specific surface

4.5. The link between fractal dimension and pore structure 
characterization parameters 
The characterization parameters of pore structure in concrete 
include pore volume, pore diameter distribution, specific surface area, 
and pore morphology, etc.. However, it is not accurate to express the 
pore structure only by a single parameter. For instance, the pore volume 
is an overall summary value to indicate the total number of pores inside 
the material, which cannot accurately describe the number and diameter 
of pores in the concrete. The pore diameter in concrete ranges from Å to 
µm, and different pore diameters have different effects on various as­
pects of the material’s properties. The most probable pore diameter is 
used to represent the cracking width of the largest number of pores in 
the concrete. It can be used to analyze the change of pore diameter. The 
pores specific surface area can only characterize the surface roughness of 
pores surface to a certain extent. Moreover, the most probable pore 
diameter and pore specific surface area are calculated under the premise 
of assumption, which only has theoretical significance. Many literatures 
[19,38] draws the conclusion that the pore structure of concrete can be 
considered as a fractal object in the aspect of pore surface areas, pore 
volumes, pore diameter, etc.. Therefore, it is very necessary to analyze 
the pore structure parameters of concrete by fractal theory. 
In Fig. 6, the concrete has fractal feature only in Region I and Region 
Ⅳ. In this paper, the relationship between the fractal dimension and the 
most probable pore diameter, porosity, and pore specific surface area in 
Region I and Region Ⅳ only is considered, and displayed in Fig. 10 and 
Fig. 11. From Fig. 10, the relationship between the pore volume and the 
fractal dimension is discretely distributed in Region I, while the distri­
bution shows a negative correlation in the Region IV. However, the 
correlation coefficient between pore volume and fractal dimension is 
0.7341, which is smaller than that of fractal dimension and the most 
probable pore diameter and pore specific surface area. This illustrates 
that the complexity of pore structure distribution is also affected by 
other factors, such as pore diameter and specific surface area, when the 
pore volume is the same. The relationship between the fractal dimension 
and the most probable pore diameter both shows a negative correlation, 
while the relationship with the specific surface area shows a positive 
correlation, in Region I and Region IV. This is cause that when the pores 
inside the concrete are mainly medium and small pores, the surface 
roughness of the pores increases, and thus the fractal dimension in­
creases. This is consistent with the fractal theory. Moreover, the corre­
lation in the Region I is less than that in the Region IV, which can be 
understood as the ability of micropores to occupy space is relatively 
strong.

development trend in each age is the same as the overall trend. In Region

4.6. Compressive strength 
Fig. 12 displays the observed values of compressive strength of C10- 
C150 concrete, to better meet the design requirements. In Fig. 12, fcu,0 
(min) and fcu,0(min)
’ are the minimum values of compounding strengths of 
C10-C50 concrete and C60-C150 at 28 days, respectively. And fcu,k is the 
standard value of concrete cube compressive strength at 28 days. It 
shows a well-known law obviously that the W/C is an important factor in 
determining strength. Concrete is heterogeneous composite materials 
composed of crystals, gels, aggregates, incompletely hydrated particles, 
free water, and pores. The action of crystal, gel and incompletely hy­
dration particles is to increase the intensity, while free water and pores is 
the opposite. When the W/C is relatively small, free water in the system 
decreases, crystals and gels increase, thereby increasing the strength of 
the concrete. When water consumption exceeds the requirements for 
cement hydration, excessive free water will migrate and bleed, which 
will produce a large number of pores in concrete. It will lead to the force- 
bearing area of the concrete specimen diminish. Meanwhile, the 
strength of the interface between the aggregate and the cementitious 
materials reduces. Hence, the compressive strength of concrete is closely 
related to the density of pores structure.

strength, many parameters need to be considered. But it will lead to the 
mathematical model expression too complex. Hence, the representative 
parameters were selected for a simple model from upper parameters.

products are gradually formed, and the packing pattern of hydrated 
cement grains is denser in the concrete, and the compressive strength is 
higher. At this time, the pores in concrete are mostly concentrated in the 
range of small pores. It will lead to pore surface area increases and 
rougher internal surface and more complex pore structure of concrete.

the larger the fractal dimension is, the more complex the spatial geo­
metric characteristic of the pore structure is. Thus, the stronger the space 
filling capacity of concrete, the higher the compressive strength. This 
proves once again that the magnitude of compressive strength is closely 
related to the compactness of microscopic pore structure. However, the 
correlation coefficient of R2 IV is greater than that of R2 I, which are 
0.9112 and 0.8823. This is because formation methods of different pores 
are different, the multifractal characteristics of concrete and the corre­

fractal dimension of Region I is always greater than Region Ⅳ. This 
reveals extremely irregular and disordered packing patterns of hydrated 
cement than the pore structure of C-S-H gels. Therefore, the relationship 
between fractal dimension and compressive strength of different pores is 
also different.

expresses the relationship between macroscopic mechanical properties 
and microscopic pore structure indexes and the way of mutual influence 
between them. However, comparing Region I and Region IV, the cor­
relation between fractal dimension and compressive strength is better in 
the latter. Therefore, it is more precise to choose the mathematical 
model in Region IV to predict the strength of denser concrete. 
5. Conclusions  
(1) The internal pores density and compressive strength of concrete 
increase with decrease of W/C or increase of curing age. The 
hydration process of concrete gradually tends to be stable, and 
the most probable pore diameter and pore volume reduced after 
curing 28 days, then the pores are mostly concentrated in the less 
harmful pores and the harmless pores, results in the slow growth 
of compressive strength of concrete. 
(2) Obvious fractal features are revealed in the internal pore struc­
ture of concrete. All fractal curves are divided into four segments 
according multi-fractal characteristics of concrete, namely Re­
gion I, Region II, Region III, and Region IV. Region I is macro­
pores fractal region (d = 10 μm ~ 950 μm), represents the 
packing patterns of hydrated cement grains, while Region II - 
Region Ⅳ is micropores fractal region (d = 0.0064 μm ~ 10 μm), 
reflects mainly the microstructure of C-S-H hydrates. Region I and 
Region Ⅳ exhibit obvious fractal features, the fractal dimension D 
is between 2 and 3. However, the fractal dimension D > 3 in 
Region II and Region III, which are non-physical from the point of 
view of surface geometry. 
(3) The mercury intrusion porosimetry is a very effective and prac­
tical method for measuring the pore diameter distribution of 
concrete. However, due to the deviation of the morphology of 
pores from the ideal pore model and the limitation of pressure 
range in the actual samples, not all the pores conform to the 
fractal features.  
(4) The internal pore characteristics of pores can be clearly described 
by fractal dimension. And fractal dimension has a good correla­
tion with the most probable pore diameter and the pore specific 
surface area. The pore volume fractal dimension can be consid­
ered as a comprehensive parameter of pore morphology and pore 
spatial distribution, compared with other pore structure charac­
terization parameters, which can accurately reflect the pore 
structure characteristics.  
(5) There is a good correlation between the fractal dimension of 
Region I and Region IV and the compressive strength, the math­
ematical model according with a positive power function rela­
tionship. Compressive strength can be inferred by comparing and 
analyzing the fractal dimension in the concrete. In practical en­
gineering, the damage detection of concrete on site may be 
realized by the mathematical model. 
6. Further research  
(1) More cement composite cementitious materials with different 
mix proportions should be considered in further, to verify and 
modify the mathematical model between the compressive 
strength and the fractal dimension.  
(2) In this test, the pore structure of concrete was measured by the 
mercury intrusion porosimetry(MIP). Further, the pore structure 
with various cement composite cementitious materials will be 
measured by different methods, such as nitrogen adsorption 
method, small angle x-ray scattering (SAXS), scanning electron 
microscope (SEM), Computed Tomography (CT) and different 
calculation models, the similarities and differences between them 
will be attempted to compare and analyze.  
(3) It is a simplified mathematical model between the fractal 
dimension and compressive strength in this experiment, and it is 
in the stage of exploration and research at present. In the future, 
the relationship between the fractal dimension and compressive 
strength will be further analyzed to establish a more accurate 
mathematical calculation model.
